Splatoon ink stays on floors and walls. How does color remain where a shot lands?

A method disclosed in a Nintendo patent links the surface to a transparent painting sheet. This color image is a texture.

First, detect where the ink hits. Convert that three-dimensional point into coordinates on a virtual plane associated with the surface.

Find the address to paint in the image: its UV coordinates. Halfway across and halfway up means the horizontal address is 0.5, and the vertical address is 0.5.

Stamp an ink shape around that address and combine it with the original wall image. Floors and walls use their corresponding surface coordinates.

A mark could break at a boundary between image patches. The neighboring virtual surfaces are connected for this processing.

Turn the camera and the ink stays in place. Color is recorded in the surface texture, rather than the screen.
