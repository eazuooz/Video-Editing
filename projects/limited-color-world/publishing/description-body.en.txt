How can a screen communicate where to go and what to interact with when colors are difficult to distinguish? We examine house outlines, plant height and motion, and contrast in dark spaces through actual Chicory gameplay.

Spatial diagrams connect these observations to separating fill from structure, adding pattern and shape, communicating current state, and keeping important boundaries readable. We also connect grayscale and color-perception previews to actual play checks of targets, states and the next action.

The device handles and background-occlusion comparisons are design examples. A preview or a single scene does not establish accessibility for every player.
