AI can produce a working program, yet a blank project can still leave you stuck. Why is finding a junior developer role difficult, and how much of that can be explained by AI alone?

We begin with hiring and the difference between understanding code and independently turning a problem into a program. We compare two ways of using AI, examine live-service changes and large existing codebases, and discuss the first steps of a developer's career.

Then we turn to a concrete practice: study a linked list or a Tetris tutorial, close the finished solution, and rebuild it from an empty project. Recognition, recall, debugging and problem solving require different kinds of practice.

The goal is not to stop using AI. It is to keep developing your own judgment while learning programming. Comments about students reflect personal teaching experience; hiring explanations and future implications are the author's interpretation.
