This spray changes a number to paint. That is Teardown's trick.

Each voxel is a tiny 3D cell. It stores a one-byte material number. This number is the material index; the material table is called a palette.

At load time, prepare the original and progressively yellower variants. Spraying consults a translation table and changes the voxel's index.

For example, replace brick index 3 with yellow-brick index 9. Its shape stays; its color changes.

The palette has limited space. There are up to 255 material slots. Add four yellow and four black shades: one original now needs nine slots.

When space runs out, merge similar colors and choose the closest available match. In the early Castle example, some surfaces turned brown or stayed unchanged.

Even a spray can hides a trade-off: less memory versus more color choices.
