All these windows in SimCity— did they build every room inside?

One technique used in this city is interior mapping. It draws the depth of a room onto a flat surface.

Look at our window from the side: the surface itself is just a flat plane. Yet the front view shows a room inside.

A pixel is a tiny point in the image. The key is the line from the camera through each window pixel. We imagine walls, a floor and a ceiling behind it, then calculate which surface that line reaches first.

If it reaches the back wall first, we sample the wall texture at that point and draw that colour in the window pixel.

Move the camera to the right, and the changing ray reveals more of the side wall. Unlike a fixed photograph, the image changes with the viewing angle.

Repeat that calculation for every pixel, and without adding room geometry, the interior gains visible depth.

You cannot walk into this virtual room. In this basic version, furniture is also a flat texture.

Use real geometry for rooms you can explore, and this calculation for windows you pass by. That saves the cost of building every room.
