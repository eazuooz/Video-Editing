Can we follow a game's target while turning the whole view less? We examine aiming and viewing in a cleaning game, then the cues for recognizing a target after the view changes in a puzzle game. These observations lead to camera choices and a way to restore the original settings.

We examine the PowerWash Simulator Aim Mode development demonstration and actual gameplay from The Talos Principle 2. Red lines identify the spray or aiming direction; other short colored markers identify posts, floor boundaries and target cues. These are explanations of visible screen relationships, not measured game-engine coordinates.

The diagrams distinguish necessary changes in viewing direction from added presentation, and explain how to make settings, targets and current orientation understandable. Development-era footage and proposed interfaces are labelled separately. Individual responses can differ.
