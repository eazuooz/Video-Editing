## Title

AI Made Eight Times the Demos — So Why Don't Players Get Better Games for Less?

## Description (body)

Three years ago Steam Next Fest had about a thousand demos; now it has more than eight thousand, and a Reddit post saying AI "absolutely ruined" the fest passed 8,800 upvotes. This video uses Next Fest as one piece of evidence and asks what those numbers changed for the people who make games, the players who choose them, and the idea-driven indie market — and what a developer should prepare for the next three years.

- Steam's AI disclosure went from refusal (2023) to "allowed if written down" (2024) to "only what players see and hear" (2026). Using AI as a tool and shipping AI output are two different things.
- GDC 2026: 36% of developers use generative AI at work, while "negative impact" answers went 18% → 30% → 52%.
- Players: eight times the choice, Steam's top-50 new-release average price flat ($21.8 → $21.4), $70/$80 big releases. The quality loss and price-to-quality burden land on players.
- Indies: an idea gets cloned the moment it is shown (14+ Needle In A Haystack clones, a 24-hour Roblox clone). Of 20,282 Steam releases in 2025, only 3% passed 1,000 reviews.
- Preparation: AI as a tool with humans owning what is on screen and honest disclosure; time where the human hand shows; execution and people as the moat, not the idea.

All footage is our own screen recording of Steam search, the Next Fest hub, store pages and public report/article pages; numbers and quotes are from the sources below. Cards marked "우리 전망" are Yamyam Coding's own view.
