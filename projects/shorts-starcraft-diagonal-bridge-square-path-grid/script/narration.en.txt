This StarCraft bridge lies diagonally. But the walkable route was calculated on a square grid.

The older engine divided terrain into thirty-two-by-thirty-two-pixel tiles. Each tile was marked passable or blocked.

In StarCraft, the artwork changed to a tilted perspective, but the underlying square grid remained. Diagonal edges cut awkwardly through those squares.

So pathfinding used smaller eight-by-eight-pixel cells. The same area now holds four cells across and four down. One tile becomes sixteen cells.

In our diagram, smaller cells let the passable boundary follow the bridge more closely. But more cells also mean more pathfinding work.

Under the smooth-looking diagonal bridge was a staircase-shaped map of where units could walk. The artwork and the map used to decide movement were different representations.
