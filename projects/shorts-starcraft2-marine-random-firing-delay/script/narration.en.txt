A group of StarCraft II Marines fires together. One detail that helps this look natural is a tiny random variation in the interval between shots.

What happens if they all fire at precisely the same interval? Starting together makes every shot stay aligned, which looks robotic.

In our demonstration, change only the wait before the next shot. The soldiers’ positions, target and animation remain unchanged.

For example, instead of always waiting one second, one soldier could wait0.98seconds and another1.02seconds. These numbers are an illustrative example.

Even if they start together, repeated shots gradually fall out of sync. Tiny randomness in shot timing makes the whole group feel more natural.

StarCraft II really does add a tiny random difference between Marine shots. Even identical units need not feel locked to one rhythm.
